# Three-minute pitch

**English** · [Español](PITCH_3_MIN_ES.md)

## 0:00–0:25 — Problem

“This place looks like Mars” sounds appealing, but it is not an engineering specification. A team testing a rover needs something more concrete: which site has conditions relevant to perception, traction, and navigation, what evidence supports that choice, and what still needs field verification. Pointing to famous analog sites such as Yungay does not demonstrate that we can search for alternatives.

## 0:25–0:50 — Solution

We built AnalogOS, a planetary analog exploration system. It does not declare that a place is Mars. It helps teams decide where to investigate for a defined test, separating three questions that are often confused: physical similarity, confidence in the evidence, and site feasibility. Our vision is global; today we demonstrate a bounded pilot in Chile and Argentina.

## 0:50–1:30 — Science

We use the Jezero region as our reference, with MOLA data from NASA PDS. We searched a grid of 50 windows, each roughly 20 kilometers across, in two test regions. We calculated NASADEM topography at a scale comparable to MOLA, about 480 meters, to prioritize the initial selection.

We then checked each window against published studies. Four contained sampling coordinates from a 2003 study and were visibly excluded from the discovery ranking. Only the six shortlisted windows received twelve monthly Sentinel-2 mosaics and NASA POWER climate context. Official geological maps now support reconnaissance questions, but geology remains outside the similarity score: we do not invent calibrated values.

## 1:30–2:15 — Results and demonstration

The three Chilean candidates score between 90 and 95 for similarity, above Yungay, which we recalculated as a known reference at 75.67. The three Argentine candidates score between 49 and 64.

Those numbers need context. None of the candidates is a confirmed new analog site: both regions have published precedents, and every site needs review. The Argentine scores are lower mainly because of coarse climate data describing a cell larger than the window. We disclose that limitation. Satellite images also show traces and structures that need to be checked against official maps and field observations.

## 2:15–2:45 — What makes it different

The system searches rather than simply listing famous places: the grid is generated by an algorithm, and every exclusion has a recorded reason. It never hides a published precedent to suggest a discovery. Scores are deterministic, confidence does not alter similarity, and feasibility is not inferred from resemblance to Mars. The manual workflow works offline with a validated snapshot. Optional AI helps draft requirements and highlight existing evidence; the user confirms the requirements and the engine evaluates them.

## 2:45–3:00 — Closing

Our next steps are field reconnaissance, geological calibration, territorial checks, local climate verification, and expansion to more regions. AnalogOS turns a visual impression into a hypothesis that can be measured and audited. It does not say “this place is Mars.” It says “investigate this place, for these reasons and within these limits.”
